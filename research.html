Unemployment Insurance Generosity and Enrollment into Postsecondary Education

Job Market Paper

This paper examines whether reductions in unemployment insurance (UI) generosity affect enrollment in postsecondary education. Between 2011 and 2016, nine U.S. states reduced the maximum duration of UI benefits. Using institution-level enrollment data from the Integrated Postsecondary Education Data System (IPEDS) from 2007–2019 and a stacked difference-in-differences design, I estimate the effect of these policy changes on postsecondary enrollment. I find that UI benefit reductions increased certificate program enrollment by approximately 5.8 percent, but had no measurable effect on degree-granting institutions. These findings suggest that reduced UI generosity may encourage displaced workers to invest in short-term occupational training.

Labor Supply Effects of Universal Licensing Recognition Laws

Occupational licensing creates barriers to entry and mobility for many occupations in the United States. In this paper, I study whether Universal Licensing Recognition laws that reduce barriers to mobility affect the number of people entering licensed professions. I find that the estimated effects of these laws are generally centered around zero.
